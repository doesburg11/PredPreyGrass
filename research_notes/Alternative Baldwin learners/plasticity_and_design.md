# Reward-modulated plasticity and experimental design

## Are there established reward-modulated plasticity algorithms suitable for this tiny linear policy that are meaningfully distinct from REINFORCE?

### Takeaway
Three-factor plasticity is an established biological/computational family, but the action-credited rule currently in the repository is already a policy-gradient eligibility trace: removing its random per-synapse multiplier yields a sound control, not a genuinely independent alternative to REINFORCE. A genuinely different plasticity candidate would need a different local credit signal (for example a shared, evolved Hebbian/Oja/BCM-style rule or perturbation-based node credit), at the cost of weaker guarantees and substantially more design freedom.

### Cited Findings
- In three-factor or “neo-Hebbian” learning, correlated pre/post activity creates a synapse-local eligibility trace and a later third factor such as reward, punishment, surprise, or novelty converts the trace into weight change; eligibility duration should roughly match the action-to-reward delay. — [Gerstner et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6079224/)
- Florian analytically derived reward-modulated STDP from reinforcement learning. His delayed-reward version stores a decaying synaptic eligibility trace, and simulations learned XOR and target output patterns. This supports eligibility traces as legitimate RL implementations, but also shows that reward-modulated plasticity need not be algorithmically independent of policy-gradient reasoning. — [Florian, 2007](https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article/19/6/1468/7190/Reinforcement-Learning-Through-Modulation-of-Spike)
- Farries and Fairhall combined performance modulation with STDP in a two-layer feed-forward network and learned multiple input-to-response mappings; they explicitly present it as a biological implementation of reinforcement learning. — [Farries & Fairhall, 2007](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/10.1152/jn.00364.2007)
- Reward-modulated Hebbian learning has also reproduced selective reorganization observed in a brain-control task, showing that global performance modulation plus local activity can produce useful credit without a per-connection learned sign matrix. — [Legenstein et al., 2010](https://motorlab.pitt.edu/pub/reward-modulated.pdf)
- Differentiable plasticity uses base weights plus per-connection plasticity coefficients and Hebbian traces; optimized plastic networks solved memorization, Omniglot, and maze exploration tasks. Crucially, its coefficients are trained across tasks, not expected to work as fresh random signs. — [Miconi, Stanley & Clune, 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/miconi18a.html)
- Backpropamine adds learned neuromodulatory gating to differentiable Hebbian plasticity and reports gains in reinforcement- and supervised-learning tasks, but its plasticity and modulation parameters are optimized by gradient descent across tasks. — [Miconi et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10585)
- The repository's current action-credited update uses `one_hot(action)-probs`, the same softmax log-policy derivative as its REINFORCE learner, accumulates it in a reward-gated trace, then applies it through `alpha * trace`. — [Repository `networks.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin_hebbian/networks.py#L106-L145)

### Inferences
- A fixed positive global coefficient with `one_hot(action)-probs` eliminates sign scrambling, but it is best described as REINFORCE with an eligibility trace, decay, and clipping. It is a valuable mechanistic control and possibly a robust replacement implementation, but not a meaningfully independent learning algorithm.
- A fixed-sign global-rate rule can be genuinely distinct only if its local eligibility is not the log-policy gradient. Plausible choices include reward-modulated pre×post correlation with Oja/BCM-style normalization or reward-modulated node perturbation. For this single-layer four-action softmax, however, plain pre×post activity does not identify the sampled action; the project's failed untargeted rule is direct evidence that this distinction may sacrifice the credit signal needed here.
- A low-dimensional shared plasticity rule is a better evolutionary hypothesis than one random coefficient per connection: evolve a handful of global terms/rates and apply them to every connection. This preserves local plasticity and lets evolution choose Hebbian versus anti-Hebbian effects without presenting each founder with dozens of independently random signs.
- If “meaningfully distinct from REINFORCE” is a hard selection criterion, three-factor plasticity should not automatically outrank SARSA(λ) or actor–critic: many principled reward-modulated rules are derivations or neural implementations of policy-gradient/TD objectives rather than independent alternatives.

### Gaps
- The literature does not identify a canonical rate-based three-factor rule for exactly a stateless, single-linear-layer, four-action softmax policy driven by `E_t-E_{t-1}`. Translating spiking STDP/Oja/BCM results requires design choices.
- No source establishes that a randomly initialized shared plasticity rule will yield useful first-generation lifetime learning in this ecology; published successes generally meta-optimize plasticity over many evaluations.

## Can reward-modulated plasticity avoid per-connection random-sign scrambling without making the comparison unfair or trivial?

### Takeaway
Yes, by fixing a positive global learning rate or evolving a small shared rule rather than an independent sign per connection. The fairest decisive treatment is a global-positive eligibility-trace control; the fairest genuinely plastic alternative is a shared low-dimensional evolved rule, with matched genome dimensionality and explicit ablations. A learned neuromodulator network is defensible but is a larger-capacity treatment, not a drop-in learner swap.

### Cited Findings
- The current effective policy is `base_weights + action_alpha_weights * hebb_trace`; founders draw every alpha independently from a zero-mean Gaussian. Thus the repository itself places an independently random sign between a coherent trace and behavior. — [Repository `networks.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin_hebbian/networks.py#L66-L82); [repository `genome.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin_hebbian/genome.py#L134-L160)
- Miconi et al. emphasize trainable, connection-specific plasticity coefficients; their evidence supports optimized coefficients, not random per-connection coefficients as a useful zero-generation learner. — [Miconi, Stanley & Clune, 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/miconi18a.html)
- Najarro and Risi evolved synapse-specific Hebbian rules across tasks; learned rules enabled random-weight agents to navigate a dynamic visual environment and a damaged quadruped to adapt rapidly without explicit within-lifetime reward. This is evidence that outer-loop optimization can shape useful local rules, not evidence that unselected random rules work. — [Najarro & Risi, NeurIPS 2020](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/ee23e7ad9b473ad072d57aaa9b2a5222-Abstract.html)
- Soltoggio et al. evolved neuromodulatory topologies for reward-varying tasks and reported superior learning to fixed-weight or ordinary Hebbian networks; modulation was broadcast to target plastic synapses. — [Soltoggio et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1109/CEC.2007.4424781)
- Risi, Hughes, and Stanley identify deception as a central difficulty in evolving adaptive networks: evolution can improve static behavior before discovering learning, and novelty search helped escape that trap in dynamic reward tasks. — [Risi, Hughes & Stanley, 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1059712310379923)
- Evolutionary search can discover free-form, interpretable plasticity rules, and the resulting work highlights interactions between plasticity and homeostatic terms rather than one universal Hebbian formula. — [Jordan et al., 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/66273)

### Inferences
- Recommended hierarchy of treatments:
  1. **Global-positive trace:** one positive scalar `η` (or separate positive/negative scalars, matching the baseline) directly multiplies the action-credited trace. This isolates random-sign scrambling with essentially no extra capacity. Label it “eligibility-trace policy gradient,” not a new Hebbian algorithm.
  2. **Evolved shared rule:** evolve a few global parameters shared by all synapses, such as learning-rate magnitude, trace decay, and coefficients on bounded pre×post, pre-only, post-only, and weight-normalizing terms. Constrain the overall rate positive (softplus/absolute-value transform), while evolved rule terms can create coherent Hebbian or anti-Hebbian direction. This avoids connection-wise random signs and is meaningfully more distinct.
  3. **Evolved neuromodulator:** a small inherited network computes a scalar gate from observation/evaluation change. This has literature precedent but adds representation and parameters; compare it only with parameter-count-matched controls and a fixed-modulator ablation.
- Fixing every alpha to `+1` is not “unfairly helping” the treatment if the scientific question is whether the trace itself can learn. It removes an untrained outer-loop parameter, analogous to choosing a positive learning rate in REINFORCE. It becomes unfair only if reported as evidence that evolution discovered plasticity.
- Conversely, giving plasticity a neuromodulator network while REINFORCE retains fixed learning rates changes both learner and model capacity. That experiment can ask whether evolved meta-learning helps, but cannot isolate which local update is superior.
- A shared evolved rule also makes assimilation interpretable: selection acts on a handful of plasticity genes, while base-policy genes can become constrained. Independent alpha at every connection doubles the action genome and confounds “policy assimilation” with “plasticity-rule optimization.”

### Gaps
- Parameter-count matching does not fully equalize inductive bias: a global-rate learner has fewer degrees of freedom but a strong helpful sign constraint, whereas connection-specific alpha has more capacity but a much harder evolutionary search.
- Published evolved-neuromodulation studies use task distributions or explicitly changing contingencies; whether this ecology supplies sufficiently repeated, informative selection pressure for a modulator is unknown.

## What evidence says evolution can shape plasticity rules in Baldwin/meta-learning settings, and what repository interfaces and controls must a replacement preserve?

### Takeaway
Outer-loop evolution can shape initial weights, local plasticity rules, and modulatory structure, but the evidence comes from deliberately meta-learning across tasks or changing contingencies. In this repository a valid replacement must keep ecology, observation/action model, innate evaluation signal, random-number schedule where practicable, Darwinian inheritance boundary, and ERL/E/L/F/B controls fixed; it must also separate base-policy and plasticity-gene metrics instead of pooling them.

### Cited Findings
- Fernando et al. evolved initial parameters that support rapid within-lifetime learning and report genetic accommodation of learning biases on the same problem sets used for MAML, directly framing the Baldwin effect as meta-learning. — [Fernando et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07917)
- Najarro and Risi evolved per-synapse local learning rules rather than network weights and demonstrated fast within-lifetime self-organization, showing that evolution can optimize plasticity itself. — [Najarro & Risi, NeurIPS 2020](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/ee23e7ad9b473ad072d57aaa9b2a5222-Abstract.html)
- The EPANN review defines evolved plastic neural networks as systems in which evolution breeds networks with plastic components that change from experience, and surveys evolution of architectures, dynamics, and learning rules. — [Soltoggio, Stanley & Risi, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30142505/)
- The baseline computes the lifetime reinforcement as the fixed inherited evaluation network's `E_t-E_{t-1}`, applies learning before the next action, and uses the same linear softmax action model for every non-Brownian condition. — [Repository `world.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin/world.py#L505-L531)
- Baseline offspring inherit the genome action weights, not the parent's lifetime-modified live action weights; learning state is reset by copying the child genome into a fresh live network. — [Repository `world.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin/world.py#L810-L858)
- The repository defines ERL (evolution+learning), E (evolution only), L (learning only, cloned genome), F (neither), and B (uniform random behavior), with ecology and carnivores otherwise shared. — [Repository `world.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin/world.py#L247-L288)
- The Hebbian fork's current functional-constraint tracker pools base action weights and alpha coefficients into one `action_site_change_rate`, even though these gene classes answer different questions. — [Repository `metrics.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin_hebbian/metrics.py#L29-L56)
- Current population diagnostics report alpha absolute magnitude and trace absolute magnitude, but not sign coherence, effective weight displacement, induced logit displacement, or alignment with useful behavior. — [Repository `world.py`, commit 3bf8443](https://github.com/doesburg11/PredPreyGrass/blob/3bf8443443c990b335c31d6fa06cff377a9df8a2/predpreygrass/evolutionary/eco_evolutionary_erl_baldwin_hebbian/world.py#L997-L1028)

### Inferences
- **Interface invariants:** preserve the observation vector and four actions; keep the inherited evaluation network lifetime-fixed; use exactly `E_t-E_{t-1}` unless reward is a separately declared factor; update only per-life learner state during action selection; reproduce only from inherited genome state; reset all traces/live deltas at birth; leave ecology, mortality, reproduction, and carnivore mechanics untouched.
- **Fairness invariants:** reuse founder genomes and environmental seeds across algorithms; match the order/timing of evaluation, update, sampling, and action resolution; do not retune ecology per learner; predeclare comparable hyperparameter budgets; run enough independent population seeds to avoid presenting a lucky evolved rule as a mechanism.
- **Minimum treatments:** for every candidate, run ERL, E, L, F, and B; add learner-specific “plasticity frozen/zeroed” and “learned state reset” ablations. For evolved rule genes, add a frozen-founder-rule treatment to distinguish rule evolution from ordinary action-weight evolution. For connection-specific rules, shuffle coefficients among connections at evaluation while preserving their distribution.
- **Minimum within-lifetime metrics:** norm of actual effective weight change; norm and action-wise direction of induced logit change on encountered observations; action entropy/argmax switches; performance with lifetime state enabled versus zeroed; reward-to-update lag; saturation/clipping fraction. Trace magnitude alone is not behavioral magnitude.
- **Minimum evolutionary metrics:** separate site-change rates for evaluation genes, base action genes, and plasticity-rule genes; distributions (not only absolute means) of shared rule parameters; generation and reproduction counts; lineage lifetime/offspring outcomes; enabled-versus-zeroed plasticity fitness gap over generations. Genetic assimilation is supported when inherited base behavior improves and dependence on lifetime plasticity falls, not merely when alpha magnitude changes.
- **Minimal staged comparison:** (1) identical-founder single-lifetime diagnostic: baseline REINFORCE, global-positive action-credited trace, and one genuinely distinct shared local rule; (2) short multi-seed population smoke test checking survival and that each learning channel acts; (3) only then a preregistered long multi-generation ERL/E/L/F/B comparison. This prevents paying for evolution before verifying that the candidate has a usable first-generation phenotype while retaining the separate question of whether evolution improves its rule.

### Gaps
- Repository comments describe `action_site_change_rate` as a direct assimilation signature, but pooled absolute parent-child mutation changes are not by themselves a complete test of genetic assimilation; a counterfactual plasticity-off evaluation is still needed.
- The appropriate experimental unit and power calculation (seed count, generations, stopping rule) require variance estimates from pilot runs and cannot be set from the literature alone.
- It remains unclear whether the project's goal prioritizes algorithmic independence from REINFORCE, biological plausibility, or strongest probability of a positive Baldwin-effect result. Those criteria rank global eligibility traces, shared evolved plasticity, and SARSA/actor–critic differently.
